Prize Lift, coupled cables

Two motors, two cables, and a prize that leans on whichever cable is nearer. A playable model of the second physics idea, with every constant on a slider.

One motor, one cableEach cable has its own motor. Nothing links the two pairs except the platform between them.
Constant force while activeHolding a motor pushes its cable up with a fixed force. Releasing it leaves the cable to the weight.
Weight leans toward the near cableThe prize loads the cables by distance. Sitting at the right wall, it puts nearly all its weight on the right cable.
Long cables, water belowThe platform can sink all the way down. Any end touching the water is an automatic drop, prize still on board.
Level start, off-center prizeThe platform begins level, but the prize starts on one side, so the two cables never carry the same load.

The model

Same push, different loads

The platform hangs from two cables. Each motor pushes with the same force when active, but the prize's weight splits between the cables by distance. The cable nearer the prize carries more, so the same push lifts it less. That asymmetry is the whole game: the operator on the heavy side has to hold longer, and the operator on the light side has to hold less than feels natural.

Try it

Lift the prize to the shelf

You run motor 1. Hold the button, or A or Space, to push your cable. Let go and the weight takes over. Get both ends above the delivery line before the clock runs out, and never let either end touch the water. Pick Solo to run both motors yourself with A and L.

Tuning

Every constant of the model. Changes apply live, even mid-lift. Force is relative to the load with the prize centered, so 1.0 means the motor exactly holds a balanced platform.

A tap fires the motor for at least the tap pulse, so quick taps count. Bots decide ten times a second on a picture 0.15 seconds old. The cooperative bot levels with you and keeps holding while it carries the prize. Careless holds at random about half the time. Snake cooperates until the platform is up, then over-pulls to roll the prize onto your side and rides just above you so it stays there. If your end gets near the water, Snake levels with you and looks helpful. This is a teaching toy, not the game's physics.

Things to try

Three feelings to check

  • Hold both at once in Solo. The light end runs away, the platform tilts, the prize rolls to the heavy wall, and the heavy end gets heavier. Watch the two needs figures in the readout drift apart.
  • Carry the prize yourself. With a cooperative partner, let your end sag until the prize rolls to your wall. Your motor now needs about two thirds of the time held to hold altitude at all. Tap at half duty and you sink no matter what the partner does.
  • Watch the Snake's receipts. Its over-pull shows up as a long hold with its end above yours. Its ride afterward looks like ordinary leveling. Decide whether the receipts alone would convict it, or whether the drop reads as your fault.

What the model does. Each end is a mass equal to half the platform plus its lever share of the prize, plus a small winch inertia. Motor on: constant force minus weight minus winch drag, capped at the motor's top speed. Motor off: weight minus drag. The prize rolls with the platform's slope, with its own drag, and bounces off the tray walls. Any end at or below the water line ends the lift. Both ends above the delivery line ends it the other way. The clock stops at 45 seconds.

What it leaves out. The Rig, the Catch, and the capsule from the brief. A rigid platform's true two-body motion, replaced by two independent ends limited to about 53 degrees of tilt. Any brake or ratchet on an idle winch. Prize spill over the walls. Defaults were picked by simulating bot pairs: two cooperative operators deliver in 8 to 12 seconds, a 60% tapper sinks whoever the partner is, and a 70% tapper delivers.
