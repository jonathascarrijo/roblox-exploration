Prize Lift, coupled cables

Two motors, two cables, and a prize that leans on whichever cable is nearer. A playable model of the second physics idea, with the Catch, the Rig, and every constant on a slider.

One motor, one cableEach cable has its own motor. Nothing links the two pairs except the platform between them.
Constant force, changing weightA held motor pushes its cable with a fixed force. The weight on that cable changes with where the prize sits. Acceleration is the difference.
Weight leans toward the near cableThe prize loads the cables by distance. Near the right end, it puts almost all its weight on the right cable.
Long cables, water belowThe platform can sink all the way down. Any end touching the water is an automatic drop, no Catch offered.
No walls, one CatchThe prize sticks below a tilt threshold, then slides, and leaves off either end. When it does, the winch freezes, the prize falls for the Catch window, and each operator gets one tap.
The Rig, doubledAn armed Rig doubles a motor's force and doubles the pull on its cable, so that end moves twice as fast both ways.

The model

Same push, different loads

The platform hangs from two cables. Each motor pushes with the same force when active, but the prize's weight splits between the cables by distance. The cable nearer the prize carries more, so the same push accelerates it less, and in this build its speed settles where push, weight, and winch drag balance. That makes the difference visible: the heavy end climbs slowly and sinks fast, the light end climbs fast and sinks slowly. The prize is not a perfect ball either. Static friction holds it still until the tilt passes a threshold, and sliding friction slows it once it moves. The operator on the heavy side has to hold longer, and the operator on the light side has to hold less than feels natural.

Try it

Lift the prize to the shelf

You run motor 1. Hold the button, or A or Space, to push your cable. Let go and the weight takes over. Get both ends above the delivery line before the clock runs out. If the prize rolls off, tap Catch once while the needle is in the zone. Pick Solo to run both motors yourself with A and L. The Rig boxes give a motor the doubled force and pull; in Snake mode the Snake arms its own.

Tuning

Every slider constant of the model. Changes apply live, even mid-lift. Force is relative to the load with the prize centered, so 1.0 means the motor exactly holds a balanced platform.

A tap fires the motor for at least the tap pulse, so quick taps count. Bots decide ten times a second on a picture 0.15 seconds old. The cooperative bot levels with you and keeps holding while it carries the prize, and catches 70% of the time. Careless holds at random about half the time and catches 40%. Snake cooperates until the platform is up, arms its Rig, then either over-pulls so the prize slides off your end or, if the prize sits on its own side, lets its end drop so the prize slides off before you can level. It taps Catch outside the zone on purpose. This is a teaching toy, not the game's physics.

Things to try

Four feelings to check

  • Hold both at once in Solo. The light end runs away, the platform tilts, the prize rolls toward the heavy end and makes it heavier. With no walls it keeps going. Watch the two needs figures drift apart before it drops.
  • Carry the prize yourself. With a cooperative partner, let your end sag until the prize sits near your end. Watch the needs figure for your motor climb as the prize gets closer, and feel how sluggishly a loaded end answers a tap. Fall below that duty and you sink no matter what the partner does.
  • Arm your own Rig in Solo. Tick motor 1 under Rig. That end now moves twice as fast both ways. See how quickly a doubled end can throw the prize off the other end, and how the receipts call out an end moving faster than a plain motor can.
  • Face the Snake. Its over-pull is fast enough to beat your counter-hold. The lift then rests on your single Catch tap, which is the same bottleneck the first mockup found. Decide whether the speed tell in the receipts is enough to convict it.

What the model does. Each end is a mass equal to half the platform plus its lever share of the prize, plus a small winch inertia. Motor on: constant force minus weight minus winch drag, limited by the motor's top speed. Motor off: weight minus drag, limited by the fall cap. The Rig multiplies that motor's force, the pull on its cable, and both speed limits. The prize holds still until the slope beats static friction, then moves with the slope against sliding friction and drag, and leaves the platform when its center passes an end. A fault freezes the winch for the Catch window while the prize falls; two needle passes, one tap per operator, one hit saves it and returns it to the center. Any end at or below the water line ends the lift with no Catch. Both ends above the delivery line ends it the other way. The clock stops at 45 seconds.

What it leaves out. The capsule and the Rig's 1.5-second arming hold from the brief. A rigid platform's true two-body motion, replaced by two independent ends limited to about 53 degrees of tilt. Any brake or ratchet on an idle winch. Whether a caught prize should return to the center or to the edge it fell from.
