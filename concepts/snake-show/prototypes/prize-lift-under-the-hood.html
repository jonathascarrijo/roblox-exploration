Snake Show · design brief v2 · problem 2

Prize Lift under the hood

Why a Snake can always force a fault, why the heist still usually fails, and what one rule change does to the odds.

The winch is fine

Each cable moves its corner within a limited reach. A weight shift sags a corner by more than the red boundary. A partner who works against you during a shift forces a fault, and their lever shows it.

The Catch is the bottleneck

One tap from the Loyal partner saves the case. A heist needs that one tap to miss, twice in three attempts. Against an average partner the Snakes win 16% of episodes.

The change

Give each Snake their own Rig attempt every act. Same Catch, same winch. Snake wins rise to 37% against average partners, and the shared-attempt machinery disappears.

The rig

Two corners, two cables, one motor that never waits

The motor raises the case for about 21 seconds through three secured heights. Each operator holds one cable. Pull raises that corner, release lets it settle. The capsule inside slides around, so one corner gets heavier for a few seconds at a time. The brief describes all of this. What it doesn't say is how far one operator can move a corner, and that number decides everything below.

Same shift, three partners

What a partner does during a shift decides the outcome

Take the same moment three times: the weight slides to your corner, so your corner sags by 1.6 and you pull all the way up. What happens next depends only on the other lever.

From fault to heist

Everything after the fault is one tap

Once you accept that a Snake can always force a fault, the heist chain is short. The Snake arms Rig privately, waits for a shift toward the partner's corner, holds Pull through it, and then hopes the partner's one Catch tap misses. Nothing else in the chain has odds worth modeling.

Whole episodes

What each option does to the Snake win rate

Simulated full episodes: three acts, random pairings, the runoff tie rule from problem 1, Loyal votes landing on a Snake 45% of the time. Snakes attempt whenever they can and stop at two heists. A healthy hidden-team win rate sits around 35% to 50%.

The two-hit Catch also helps, but it breaks the brief's one-tap rule, drops innocent novice pairs to a 69% save rate, and needs a two-segment meter on a phone. It stays in reserve.

The change

One attempt per Snake, and nothing else moves

Edits to the brief, if you agree

  1. State the winch model. Each cable moves its corner within a limited reach. A load shift sags a corner by more than the red boundary. Correcting it needs the loaded corner pulled and the other corner released. A partner who holds Pull through a shift toward the other corner forces a fault, visibly.
  2. Schedule at least one shift toward each corner in every lift, so both operators must act and every Snake gets a window.
  3. One Rig attempt per Snake per act. Remove the reservation, priority, and teammate status rules. Keep the private "Rig armed" confirmation.
  4. Early Loyal victory triggers when completed heists plus remaining attempts, counting only living Snakes, can't reach two.
  5. Vary the Catch needle speed per fault among a small fixed set of patterns, to narrow the gap between novice and practiced hit rates. Window width stays a test setting.
  6. Replace the acceptance wording "if experienced players stop every heist, revise" with a measurable target: heist success per attempt between 40% and 60% against average partners.
