# Shared challenge rules

10 September 2026 · Current mechanical baseline and requirements for candidates

The [main brief](../midnight-fair-design-v3.md) governs team assignment, rounds, voting, victory, participation, and the one-baby team reward. This page defines the common challenge contract. [Individual challenge pages](README.md) supply physical controls, Rig effects, reset locations, and tests. A candidate's stated counterplay gap remains unresolved even when it is listed here.

## Terms and comparable stations

A **station** is one pair's instance of a challenge. A **job** is one player's assigned control responsibility, independent of their secret role. **Cargo** means the protected creature or its transport; **fault** means leaving safe support and triggering Catch; **loss** means an unrescued fault. A **checkpoint** is the recovery location specified by the challenge. A **diversion** is the secret rerouting of armed cargo after an unrescued fault.

Every station in a trial has exactly two jobs and comparable layout, settings, controls, starting conditions, time, and clue coverage. Jobs are assigned independently of roles. When the cast is odd, the [spotter rule](../midnight-fair-design-v3.md#the-spotter) applies; a third operator is never added to a station. Every family needs a marked rescue area.

Different jobs may exchange at a checkpoint or alternate between trials where the schedule allows. A midpoint exchange alone cannot establish equal exposure: an unsuccessful pair may never reach it. Compare arming access, difficulty, blame, and time in each job. Test two Keepers, a Trickster in either job, two Tricksters sharing their team attempt, and spotter involvement.

## Honest cooperation and delivery

Define a satisfying normal action for each player before relying on sabotage for tension. The inherited candidate targets are about 12–20 seconds for a clean run and about 25–30 seconds including a fault and recovery, within the provisional 45-second challenge budget. These are comparison targets, not established completion times.

A station banks at most one delivery per trial. Partial progress, handoffs, resets, and replacement cargo do not count as additional deliveries. Delivery is a round performance count, not a persistent baby award. Finishing players may observe or rescue another station.

The normal controls must allow a readable response to a partner's ordinary error. Each candidate must describe four steps: the ordinary error, the partner's normal counter, the Rig effect that makes it harder to counter, and the last-resort Catch. An attentive partner should have meaningful prevention and rescue opportunities. A job that can force an ordinary loss without those opportunities is a design gap, not an accepted exception.

## Rig reservation and arming

The Tricksters share one armed attempt per trial, including a Trickster spotter. Starting a private Rig hold reserves it; the first accepted reservation gets priority. Canceling before completion releases it. Completing the hold consumes it for the entire team, whether or not the eventual diversion succeeds. The teammate sees the state privately.

The inherited hold is 1.5 seconds. During the hold, the relevant ordinary job input continues, so the public action is ordinary. Releasing early, leaving the eligible job or rescue area, or a fault before completion cancels the hold. Every job's actual arming access must be specified: Vault Volley's receiving job, for example, does not yet have its own Rig control.

Completion arms the specific cargo's concealed route. The challenge also supplies a temporary physical effect of up to five seconds, such as Moonmop Lift's doubled motor response. The effect can be on the operator's control or a clearly described station-wide system. It must exist in the one shared physical scene; a clue does not expose the private button that caused it.

Ending the temporary effect does not clear the armed cargo. Handoffs, job swaps, and checkpoints also do not clear it. Successful Catch, safe delivery, loss, timeout, a terminal lower-boundary failure where applicable, or cancellation resolves the armed state. It never transfers to replacement cargo. A consumed attempt is never refunded by a reset.

Only armed cargo followed by an unsuccessful Catch creates a diversion. An ordinary unarmed loss, idle time, an expired challenge, or a lower-boundary failure cannot create one by itself.

## Spotter arming

A Trickster spotter can reserve and complete Rig inside a station's rescue area using the same hold and shared team attempt. Leaving the area, releasing, or a fault during the unfinished hold cancels it. Completion arms that cargo with **no physical effect** on either operator's controls.

The operators must still cause a fault through their actual play, and somebody can still Catch. This keeps their observable handling honest. The spotter retains their own Catch opportunity and cannot be present in two rescue areas at once. Assigning the spotter and counting their vote remain round rules in the main brief.

## Catch and recovery

Use the inherited three-second, two-pass timing sequence when comparing families. The target offers a proposed 0.25-second opportunity per pass. The challenge pauses during Catch. Each operator and each eligible active player in that station's marked rescue area gets one fresh tap per fault, including the spotter.

One correctly timed tap resolves a save for everyone. Another player's miss cannot cancel it or consume a teammate's opportunity. Pre-held controls and repeated tapping cannot substitute for a fresh timed press; a miss uses that player's one opportunity for the fault.

A save clears the rig and restores the cargo to the location specified by its challenge. That page must explain which progress, job assignments, positions, and timers are retained. If nobody saves, armed cargo goes to the concealed Trickster route and unarmed cargo goes to ordinary recovery, with identical public failure presentation. A fresh unarmed cargo may restart after the proposed three-second reload if time remains. The station still has only one scoring delivery and the team still has only one consumed or available attempt.

An isolated test can restrict rescuers only if its comparison uses the same condition. Challenge variants must not quietly make one family's Catch easier than another's. If Catch overwhelms every preceding interaction as the sole determinant of results, reconsider the common recovery design before adding content.

## Clues and the shared scene

Each station supplies three cards: outcome, the longest sustained relevant handling interval or the worst handoff, and the last Catch response. The family defines its handling vocabulary and selection rule. Cards stay in fixed station order; an odd-cast trial adds the spotter's longest-stay and rescue card.

Clues report observable actions and state. They never report secret roles, private Rig input, routing, force multipliers, speed, brightness, or gust strength. Public physical effects may still be noticed live. Rigged and innocent faults use the same visible warning and failure. Successful diversion totals are announced for the trial as a whole, not exposed through one station's outcome card.

The full finale can reconstruct recorded arming, control inputs, rescue, routing, and ballots after roles are revealed. Neither clues nor replays establish an unrecorded intention or what somebody said in chat.

## Reward boundary and validation

Use the [central collection rule](../midnight-fair-design-v3.md#8-collection-rewards-and-rarity): one individual baby per participating winning team member per round, including voted-out players. A personal delivery, Catch, or diversion is not an extra eligibility condition. The baseline round's challenge determines the reward species. Shared color odds are still a proposal in v3; a challenge page must not independently settle them.

Before advancing a candidate, test ordinary refusal, permanent holds, repeated safe resets, simultaneous rescue, reservation contention, both asymmetric jobs, and the spotter. Check instruction time, actual evidence, and voluntary repeat play. Mark values as inherited defaults, untested proposals, or observed results with their limits.
