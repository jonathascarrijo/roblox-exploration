## Decision status

**Agreed:** one baby per participating member of the winning episode team, including voted-out players; no personal delivery or heist prerequisite. The user also agreed to the lobby companion, Present, reactions with creature responses, creature gatherings, three-baby showcase, and portrait nook. Animation, controls, and timing details remain subject to design and testing.

**User proposal:** an expandable catalogue of badges, with the player choosing one for persistent display while walking around the lobby.

**External ideas submitted for evaluation:** weight color odds by clean delivery, mid-air Catch, or Snake diversion; or award a dusk-colored Moonmop specifically to winning Snakes through the concealed-channel story. Neither is approved by being quoted for discussion.

## Badges: a strong way to express identity

Recommend one equipped badge next to the player's lobby name, using a compact icon and short title. Selecting the player shows what the badge means and the exact requirement. The equipment choice persists across sessions and can be changed or hidden at any time. Persistent display does not mean an irreversible selection.

An expandable catalogue allows new kinds of accomplishments over time. Start with a small recognizable set and add badges when there is a distinct achievement to describe. An arbitrarily large number of near-identical icons could make the display less understandable. This is an in-game cosmetic proposal, not a claim about any external badge service's limits.

Give badges equal display space so a new player's affectionate choice can look deliberate beside a veteran's difficult achievement. The chosen baby and badge are independent: owning a common Moonmop does not prevent wearing a badge earned through a different collection. Display is scoped to the lobby, as requested.

### Illustrative badge families

| Working badge | What it records | Why it could be worth choosing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First Friend** | Earn your first baby through an episode victory. | A small milestone with sentimental value. |
| **Moonmop Devotee** | Own a named, fixed set of Moonmop colors simultaneously. | A favorite-species identity. |
| **Lavender Gathering** | Own a specified lavender variant from each of several named species simultaneously. | A taste-driven collection across species. |
| **Both Sides of the Moon** | Win an episode as a Loyal and an episode as a Snake while eligible for the team reward. | Experience with both sides of the contest. |
| **Moonlit Menagerie** | Own a specified set of distinct species simultaneously. | Collection breadth without weighting luck by rarity. |

Names and requirements are proposals. Collection sets must identify exact variants; later roster additions must not silently change an already published requirement. Existing role assignments remain private and temporary. Wearing a badge reflects past activity, never the next match's assigned role.

### Permanent achievement, current collection

Recommend that an earned badge stays unlocked after the player trades away a qualifying baby. This revises pass 2's earlier proposal that collection titles disappear when current holdings fall below a threshold. A badge can truthfully say **“Completed this set”**, while the collection book says **“Currently owns these variants.”** Date of achievement and a clear requirement help viewers understand the difference.

Collection badges accept traded creatures; duplicates do not fill additional variant slots. A group can lend or circulate creatures so several people complete a set. Under this recommendation, that cooperation is accepted, and a completed-set badge is not proof of independent acquisition or present ownership. If a future badge is intended to prove personal earning, its requirement must explicitly count that player's episode rewards and say so in its description. Do not quietly mix the two meanings.

Badges remain with the player who achieved them and are not part of creature exchanges. Reactions, likes, petting volume, trade count, and intentionally generated faults should not become badge counters. Those actions are easy to repeat without the accomplishment we want to celebrate.

## Idea 1: weight color odds by clean delivery, Catch, or diversion

**Judgment: keep this out of the initial reward rules.** It makes the route to a supposedly better baby depend on actions that can conflict with winning cooperatively.

- If a Catch improves color odds, players gain a reason to create a recoverable fault deliberately. A Loyal might manufacture the danger they then resolve.
- If clean delivery improves odds, a novice partner or a legitimate Snake attack can reduce a Loyal's expected reward quality despite that Loyal doing the right thing and winning the episode.
- If diversion gives better rarity odds, the randomly assigned Snake role gains a reward-quality advantage. If worse, that role receives a penalty for succeeding at its objective.

The attribution is also unclear under one baby per episode. An episode can include a clean delivery in one act, a save in another, and a heist elsewhere. It needs a rule for whether odds follow the individual, station, best act, or whole team. Individual weighting would restore a version of the personal-action distinctions we just removed, including the spotter and shared-attempt problems. Team-wide weighting still allows players to manufacture whichever event improves the roll.

If outcomes merely favor different equally rare hues, rather than higher rarity, the incentive is weaker but still present: someone pursuing the Catch-associated color could intentionally create a Catch. The choice of a cosmetic goal can change how people play even without a stronger item.

There is a good idea inside the proposal: memorable play should remain attached to the collection experience. A factual origin entry can record the reward's episode result, and an actual saved lift can appear in that episode's existing finale. Do not claim every awarded baby was the exact transported creature or that every winner personally made the save. The accepted reward is an adoption earned by a team victory.

## Idea 2: a dusk palette for winning Snakes

**Judgment: strong visual storytelling; exclusivity is a separate decision.** The concealed channel opening into a nursery lit by dusk gives the Snake ending a distinctive affectionate identity. Each eligible Snake must receive their one baby, including a voted-out teammate. The particular player who armed a pod does not receive an additional or superior award.

A guaranteed dusk-colored baby for every Snake victory would need a new rule for color variety. Is dusk a single color or a family of several colors? A single guaranteed color removes the color reveal's uncertainty for those wins. A dusk family preserves variation but divides the catalogue by team role. Neither interpretation follows automatically from the current shared reward proposal.

### Supply and role preference

The current cast has six Loyals and two Snakes. With all participants eligible and equal team win rates, two episodes on average produce six Loyal-origin babies and two Snake-origin babies: **three Loyal-origin awards for every Snake-origin award**. This compares aggregate origin pools; it does not establish the rarity of any particular shade within either pool.

More generally, if the Snake team wins a fraction `s` of episodes, the expected awards per episode are `2 × s` for Snakes and `6 × (1 − s)` for Loyals, under those participation assumptions. Equal palette sizes and identical color odds would not produce equal quantities across exclusive team pools at a 50% team win rate.

Exclusive dusk colors could make exchanging useful. They could also make players seeking that palette prefer a particular randomly assigned role or leave when they do not receive it. These are incentives to investigate, not observed player behavior. Cosmetic equality does not mean every player values each palette equally.

### Recommended baseline and a possible later comparison

**Baseline recommendation:** both winning teams draw from the same color pool with the same rarity odds. Dusk is one available aesthetic. The Snake finale uses dusk lighting and its hidden nursery, and the baby's origin entry records **“Earned through a Snake victory.”** Loyal rewards record the corresponding Loyal victory. The entry stays factual through an exchange and does not imply that the new owner personally earned it. Keep public provenance focused on the event rather than a chain of player identities.

If role-linked color identity proves particularly appealing, compare a softer alternative: first select rarity with identical odds for both teams, then modestly favor dawn-style shades for Loyals and dusk-style shades for Snakes within that rarity. Every color remains obtainable by either team, and the odds are explained. This would still create some role preference and adds reward complexity; it is an optional comparison, not an adopted change or a guarantee of equal desirability.

Do not implement both performance weighting and palette bias in the same first comparison. It would be difficult to determine whether participants are responding to rarity, appearance, role, or the qualifying action.

## Next design comparison

Show a small badge catalogue with exact requirements and let players equip one beside a favorite baby. Include a simple early badge and a completed collection badge with equally polished presentation. Ask what they think each badge proves, including after an exchange.

Compare the same Snake finale with a shared-pool dusk baby and a role-exclusive dusk family. Explain the six-to-two cast, the team reward, and the availability of exchanging before asking which role participants want and why. A strong visual preference alone should not be mistaken for evidence that role-exclusive acquisition improves the game.

The current recommendation is permanent selectable achievement badges, a truthful book of current holdings, and equal color odds across winning teams. Outcome-specific stories can enrich the finale without introducing a personal reward-quality contest.
