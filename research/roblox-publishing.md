# Publishing a Roblox game for players under 16

Last verified: 5 September 2026

## Bottom line

Publishing a private Roblox experience is free and mechanically simple. A normal
personal account can also publish publicly to age-checked players aged 16 and
older at no charge after completing Roblox's account and content checks.

Reaching players under 16 requires additional verification and either Roblox
Plus/Premium tenure or a refundable Robux fee. A creator who needs a predictable
all-ages launch can instead place a 100,000 Robux deposit for expedited review.
This is a viable route for a solo developer who can leave roughly USD 840 inside
the Roblox economy, but it does not buy discovery, advertising, or approval.

Roblox says the Kids and Select framework is actively evolving. Confirm the live
terms in **Creator Hub > Audience > Reach** before paying either fee.

## Audience tiers

Roblox currently divides age-checked accounts into these relevant tiers:

| Audience | Approximate age | Eligible content maturity |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Roblox Kids | 5–8 | Minimal or Mild |
| Roblox Select | 9–15 | Minimal, Mild, or Moderate |
| Standard Roblox | 16+ | All rated content except Restricted; Restricted is 18+ |

Age ranges can vary by region. A Moderate game can reach ages 9–15 but not the
youngest Kids accounts. Some designs have higher minimum ages regardless of
their tone. For example, primarily social-hangout experiences and unrestricted
free-form user creation are not eligible for young audiences.

For Snake Show, the intended 13–16 audience crosses the Select/standard boundary.
Reaching the 13–15 part of that audience therefore requires Kids and Select
eligibility, even if the game is not intended for young children.

## Mandatory account and game preparation

For public 16+ access, the owner must have an account in good standing that is at
least two days old, complete an age check, and submit the game's Content Maturity
& Compliance questionnaire.

For Kids and Select access, the individual owner—or the owner of the community
that owns the game—must also:

- Verify through government ID or a linked parental account.
- Enable two-factor authentication.
- Keep the questionnaire accurate as the game changes.
- Meet the fee/subscription and game-evaluation requirements described below.

A personal account is sufficient. A company, community, DevEx membership, or
special developer account is not required.

## Costs in US dollars

These estimates use US web/desktop pricing before applicable tax. Roblox can vary
packages, regional prices, and checkout options.

| Route | Platform charge | Approximate USD cost | Timing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Private publishing | None | USD 0 | Immediate |
| Public 16+ publishing | None | USD 0 | After account checks/questionnaire |
| Roblox Plus qualification | USD 4.99/month | USD 9.98 for two months | Must remain active for two consecutive months |
| Standard publishing deposit | 1,000 Robux per game | About USD 9.99 | Game still completes the organic evaluation path |
| Expedited review deposit | 100,000 Robux per game | Roughly USD 840 from zero | Review takes up to 48 hours |

Roblox Premium stopped accepting new subscribers on 30 April 2026, although an
eligible existing Premium subscription can still satisfy the subscription route.
Roblox Plus is the current USD 4.99/month option.

Purchasing Robux through the web or desktop generally provides better value than
buying through an iOS, Android, or console store. The exact cost of accumulating
100,000 Robux must be calculated from the packages shown at checkout.

## The ordinary under-16 path

Without expedited review, a new game initially reaches age-checked users aged 16
and older and Trusted Friends. Roblox observes genuine engagement and performs a
safety review before admitting the game to the Kids and Select catalogs.

Roblox describes qualifying players using signals such as account tenure,
playtime in the game, play history, and recent platform spending. A player does
not need to spend money inside the game itself. This makes the engagement hurdle
hard to manufacture and can make the time to under-16 eligibility unpredictable
for a developer without an existing audience.

Roblox's current documentation is inconsistent about the precise threshold. The
Kids and Select overview still mentions 250 qualifying unique plays within a
60-day window, while the newer publishing page refers to maintaining 25 highly
engaged players in its fee and refund rules. The live Audience Reach dashboard
should be treated as controlling.

## The expedited-review path

Expedited review is the alternative for a creator who needs the game evaluated
for younger audiences before meeting the organic engagement requirement.

### Submission

1. Finish ID or parental verification, enable 2FA, and ensure the account is in
   good standing.
2. Publish a release-candidate build and complete the Content Maturity &
   Compliance questionnaire accurately.
3. Place 100,000 Robux in the account that owns the game.
4. Open the game in Creator Hub and navigate to **Audience > Reach**.
5. Select expedited review and confirm payment of 100,000 Robux.

The expedited deposit substitutes for the 1,000 Robux publishing fee or the
two-month subscription requirement; it is not an additional charge on top of
them. Roblox describes the fee as per game, rather than per submitted build.

### Review outcome

Roblox says expedited review takes up to 48 hours. Passing makes the game
available to the younger tiers allowed by its maturity label, bypassing the need
to first meet the organic engagement threshold.

If reviewers find a minor violation, the creator can correct it and resubmit
without forfeiting the deposit. The 48-hour clock restarts at resubmission. A
permanent removal for a severe Community Standards violation can forfeit the
entire 100,000 Robux.

For that reason, the submitted build should be a thoroughly checked release
candidate. All images, audio, meshes, metadata, monetization, user interaction,
and imported models should be reviewed for policy and rating implications before
payment. A rushed placeholder or an unsafe free model can create unnecessary
risk.

### Deposit and refund

The deposit remains with Roblox for at least 90 days. After that period:

- If the game is in good standing and has met the required organic-engagement
  benchmark, the creator can request the Robux back and retain Kids/Select reach.
- If it has not met that benchmark, the creator can leave the deposit in place
  to maintain Kids/Select reach.
- A severe violation that permanently removes the game can make the deposit
  non-refundable.

The refund is economically important: Roblox returns Robux, not necessarily the
US dollars originally used to buy them. Directly purchased Robux are excluded
from the Developer Exchange program. A creator who buys 100,000 Robux for this
purpose should therefore treat roughly USD 840 as permanently converted into
Roblox platform currency, even if the deposit later returns to the account.
Returned Robux can still fund Roblox ads, marketplace purchases, later fees, and
other platform spending.

Roblox does not clearly document whether Robux that were originally earned retain
their Earned Robux/DevEx status after being returned from this deposit. Do not
base the decision on being able to cash out the refund later without first
obtaining confirmation from Roblox.

## What expedited review does not provide

Expedited review provides review timing and audience eligibility. It does not:

- Guarantee that the game passes review.
- Relax the Community Standards or content-maturity rules.
- Guarantee Home, search, Charts, or Recommended placement.
- Supply players, concurrent users, revenue, or advertising impressions.
- Guarantee a launch within 48 hours if the build needs corrections.
- Protect later updates from moderation or eliminate the duty to update the
  maturity questionnaire.

The route makes the most sense when the game genuinely depends on ages 5–15, the
creator already has a launch or acquisition plan, and 100,000 Robux can be left
as platform working capital indefinitely. It is less useful when the launch plan
assumes that eligibility alone will cause Roblox's recommendation system to
provide traffic.
